Slow-Braised Short Rib Ragù with Pappardelle

Melt-in-your-mouth beef simmered with Chianti, San Marzano tomatoes, and fresh garden herbs over silky ribbons of egg pasta.

Preparation Timeline

Step-by-Step
01

Sear the Beef

High Heat • Cast Iron / Dutch Oven

Pat the short ribs completely dry with paper towels and season aggressively with kosher salt. Heat olive oil until shimmering, then sear ribs without crowding until a deep, dark mahogany crust forms on all sides. Transfer to a resting plate.

visibilityVisual cue: Deep mahogany crust
02

Build the Soffritto

Medium-Low • Aromatic Base

Pour off all but two tablespoons of rendered beef fat. Add diced onion, carrot, and celery. Sweat slowly until softened and lightly golden. Stir in crushed garlic for the final 60 seconds until fragrant.

03

Deglaze with Red Wine

Medium-High • Reduce Fond

Pour in 1 cup of dry Chianti, scraping every caramelized bit of fond from the bottom with a flat wooden spoon. Bring to a rapid simmer and reduce the liquid volume by half until glossy and concentrated.

visibilityVisual cue: Scrape fond & reduce wine
04

The Long Simmer

Gentle Simmer • Covered Braise

Nestle ribs back into the pot with collected juices. Add crushed San Marzano tomatoes and the bundled herbs. Cover tightly with a heavy lid, reduce heat to the lowest setting, and braise gently until meat collapses at the touch of a fork.

05

Toss & Emulsify

Assembly • Finishing Touch
6m

Discard herb bundle and bones. Coarsely shred the tender meat directly into the thick sauce. Boil pappardelle 2 minutes shy of packaging directions, then transfer straight into ragù with 1/2 cup starchy pasta water. Toss vigorously with Parmigiano-Reggiano until lustrous.

visibilityVisual cue: Glossy, emulsified ribbons

Chef's Culinary Story

"This ragù embodies traditional Florentine Sunday lunches where the house fills with the intoxicating perfume of seared beef and wild rosemary from midday until dusk. The secret lies in never rushing the fond deglaze—that caramelized sediment provides 80% of your sauce's depth."

— Chef Marco V. Tuscan Culinary Heritage

Sommelier Wine Pairing

Chianti Classico Riserva DOCG Top Pick

High natural acidity cuts cleanly through the rich, unctuous marrow and braised collagen of short ribs while echoing the dried cherry and thyme aromas.

Langhe Nebbiolo or Barbaresco Alternative

Tannic backbone and savory truffled earth notes harmonize with slow-reduced tomato paste and Parmigiano-Reggiano.

skillet

Recommended Equipment

5.5 qt Enameled Cast Iron Dutch Oven (Le Creuset or Staub) for even thermal retention, plus a wide wooden flat spatula for scraping the foundational fond without scratching enamel.

kitchen

Make-Ahead & Storage Advice

This ragù actually improves overnight as gelatinous connective tissue settles. Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days in the refrigerator, or freeze in batches for up to 3 months. Reheat slowly with a splash of hot water.
